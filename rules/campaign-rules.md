# Campaign Rules

*A single battle is forgotten by morning. A campaign remembers: the dead stay dead, the wounded limp into the next fight, and every Victory Point becomes Gold. This chapter explains how to link Missions into a campaign, what happens to your models after each battle, and how to spend Gold in the Grimheim Hub.*

## Core Campaign Rules

**At a glance**

- Agree on a Points value. Every Point you spend on models and equipment is 1 Gold invested.
- After each Mission your Victory Points become Gold: 20 Gold per VP, 30 Gold per VP for the Underdog.
- Between Missions run the After Battle Sequence: injuries, recovery, then a visit to the Hub.
- A model never has more than 5 Traits. Traits granted by equipment do not count.

Campaigns turn single skirmishes into a running story in which your warband grows, suffers setbacks and forges its legend across linked battles. Grimheim offers three campaign formats: Ongoing Campaigns for open-ended play with any number of players, Conquest Campaigns for a two-player war over territory, and Map Control Campaigns for a war over the districts of Grimheim itself. The rules in this section apply to every format.

### Starting Resources

Before the campaign begins, the players agree on a common Points value for building their starting warbands. In a campaign, Points and Gold are one system: every Point spent on models or equipment equals 1 Gold in value. Your starting warband's point cost is therefore your first investment in the campaign.

#### Build your warband

Build your starting warband within the agreed point limit, remembering that each point spent is Gold invested. Models recruited later in the campaign follow the same principle: their point value is their Gold cost. See Building a Warband for the complete construction rules, faction options and model costs.

### Victory Rewards

**Key rule**

After each Mission, the Victory Points (VP) you earned convert to Gold at a fixed rate of **20 Gold per VP**. The Underdog converts at **30 Gold per VP** instead.

**Underdog:** a player who received at least 5 VP fewer than any other player in the previous Mission is the Underdog for this Mission.

Gold is your main resource for developing your warband between battles. Spend Gold to:

- recruit new models at their point value cost;
- purchase equipment and upgrades;
- heal injuries at the Hub's various locations;
- access faction-specific services and training.

Conquest Campaigns also award Conquest Points (CP) for territorial conquest. CP decide the campaign separately from Gold earnings (see Conquest Campaign).

Example Gold from Victory Points, with the Underdog

Player A and Player B start a campaign. In the first Mission Player A scores 3 VP and Player B scores 9 VP. Neither player is the Underdog yet.

1.  Mission 1: A earns 3 × 20 = 60 Gold. B earns 9 × 20 = 180 Gold.
2.  A received 6 VP fewer than B. That is at least 5 fewer, so A is the Underdog for Mission 2.
3.  Mission 2: A scores 7 VP and B scores 5 VP. As the Underdog, A converts at 30 Gold: 7 × 30 = 210 Gold. B earns 5 × 20 = 100 Gold.
4.  For Mission 3 nobody is the Underdog: A scored more than B, and B trailed by only 2 VP.

After two Missions A has earned 270 Gold and B 280 Gold, although B scored 14 VP to A's 10.

### Side Quests

Side Quests are optional objectives that can be undertaken during Missions for additional Victory Points and narrative depth. See Side Quests.

In a campaign every extra VP is also 20 Gold (or 30 for the Underdog).

### The Campaign Cycle

Between Missions your warband determines the lasting consequences of the battle. The After Battle Sequence has three steps:

1.  **Determine lasting consequences.** Roll on the injury tables for Defeated or seriously wounded models.
2.  **Heal and recover.** Restore wounds and remove temporary effects.
3.  **Visit the Hub.** The warband returns to its faction's Hub to spend earned Gold on new models, upgrades or special benefits.

### Campaign End

- **Ongoing Campaign:** the campaign ends on an agreed date, or never. The player with the most ranking points wins: 3 for a win, 1 for a draw, 0 for a loss. Total Victory Points break ties.
- **Conquest Campaign:** the campaign ends when one player captures the Enemy Stronghold (immediate victory), or when both players agree to conclude. In that case the highest Conquest Points determine the winner.

### Campaign Legacy

A campaign's end need not end your warband's story. Veteran warbands may continue into new campaigns with their accumulated experience, equipment and battle-hardened warriors, creating stories that span several campaigns.

Alternatively, successful warbands can retire with honour, their legends told in the taverns and halls of Grimheim, while you begin afresh with a new warband and new challenges.

### Traits Limit

Additional Traits can only be gained during a Campaign if the total of 5 is not exceeded. This counts every Trait from Body Type, Tier, Survivor results and training; traits granted by equipment do not count (see The Model Card).

## Campaign Types

**At a glance**

- Ongoing: any number of players, any agreed Mission, ranking by wins.
- Conquest: two players in a tug of war over five zones toward the enemy Stronghold.
- Map Control: two to six players on a map of Grimheim's districts. Rules in preparation.

The three formats differ in structure and victory conditions.

| Format | Players | Structure | Victory | Best for |
|----|----|----|----|----|
| **Ongoing** | Any number; players join and leave | Any agreed Mission; every warband keeps a Roster | Most ranking points (win 3, draw 1, loss 0) | Open groups, campaign nights with changing players, long-running narratives |
| **Conquest** | Two | Five zones, from your Stronghold to the enemy's | Capture the Enemy Stronghold, or most CP when both players conclude | Epic two-player campaigns with dramatic escalation and territorial conquest |
| **Map Control** | Two to six | A map of tiles held through a control mechanism | In preparation | Groups who want a shared map and a long game |

### Ongoing Campaign

An Ongoing Campaign links any number of Missions into an open-ended story. Players join and leave while the campaign runs, warbands grow between battles, and nobody has to be present for every game.

#### Campaign structure

- **Missions.** Players play any Mission they agree on. Consequences carry forward between battles: injuries, equipment and warband changes.
- **Roster.** Every warband keeps a Roster of all its models, equipment and Gold. Before each Mission the player picks a warband from the Roster up to the agreed mission limit (600 points unless agreed otherwise). Models left in the Roster earn no Gold and suffer no injuries. A Roster has no maximum; the mission limit bounds each game.
- **Joining and leaving.** A player who joins a running campaign builds a warband worth the average Roster value of the other players, at most the mission limit plus 100 Gold. Leaving freezes the Roster; a returning player continues with the frozen Roster.
- **Pairing.** Any player may challenge any other, but nobody plays the same opponent three times in a row, and the player with the fewest games played challenges first. When everyone is at the table, pair players with equal numbers of wins and resolve the rest with a Roll-off. If two Rosters differ by more than 100 points, the weaker player chooses the Mission.

#### Victory conditions

- The campaign ends on an agreed date, or never.
- Ranking by wins: 3 points for a win, 1 for a draw, 0 for a loss. Total Victory Points break ties.

Example A new player joins an Ongoing Campaign

The group plays with a mission limit of 600 points. Three players already take part, with Roster values of 780, 690 and 630 points.

1.  The average Roster value of the other players is (780 + 690 + 630) ÷ 3 = 700.
2.  The cap is the mission limit plus 100 Gold: 600 + 100 = 700. The average does not exceed it, so the new player builds a warband worth 700. Had the Rosters averaged 760, the new player would still build only 700.
3.  Before each Mission the new player picks models from the Roster up to 600 points. The 100 points left behind earn no Gold and suffer no injuries in that Mission.
4.  Later the 630-point player meets the 780-point player. Their Rosters differ by 150 points, more than 100, so the 630-point player chooses the Mission.

The newcomer owns as much as the average veteran, but fights at the same 600 points as everyone else.

### Conquest Campaign

Two warbands fight along five zones toward each other's Stronghold. The full rules follow in Conquest Campaign and Conquest Missions and Defender Bonuses.

### Map Control Campaign (in preparation)

A war for the districts of Grimheim. The city becomes a campaign map of tiles that grant different advantages, held through a control mechanism. Two to six players.

Best for groups who want a shared map and a long game. The full rules are in preparation and follow in a later edition.

## Conquest Campaign

**At a glance**

- Two warbands fight along five zones. Every campaign starts at Neutral Grounds.
- The winner of a battle moves the campaign one zone toward the enemy Stronghold; the loser falls back one zone.
- Every win earns Conquest Points (CP). Capturing the Enemy Stronghold wins the campaign at once.
- On home ground the Defender gets Defender Bonuses.

A Conquest Campaign simulates territorial warfare: a back-and-forth struggle for control of Grimheim. Two warbands fight through escalating zones, from Neutral Grounds through Enemy Territory, with the ultimate goal of capturing and destroying the enemy's Stronghold.

### Campaign Structure

- Territorial progression through five zones: Your Stronghold, Your Territory, Neutral Grounds, Enemy Territory, Enemy Stronghold.
- Victory pushes into enemy territory; defeat forces a retreat toward your own Stronghold.
- Defenders gain powerful bonuses when fighting on home ground.
- Before the campaign begins, each player selects one Stronghold Mission (faction-tagged) and three Territory Missions.
- In addition to Victory Points, Conquest Campaigns award Conquest Points (CP) to track how well you fare during the campaign.

#### Victory conditions

- Capture and destroy the Enemy Stronghold for immediate victory, **or**
- have the highest Conquest Points when both players agree to conclude.

### The Territorial System

![](media/image/dg_conquest.png)

**Fig. 6.1** The Conquest track, seen from your side. Every campaign starts at Neutral Grounds. A win at the Enemy Stronghold ends the campaign at once; a successful Stronghold defence sends the fight back into the defender's Territory, and the defender picks that Territory Mission.

#### Your Stronghold / Enemy Stronghold

- The ultimate defensive position and seat of power.
- Heavily fortified, with powerful defender bonuses.
- Falling here means campaign defeat.
- Accessed only after pushing through all previous zones.
- You are always the Defender in your Stronghold and the Attacker in the Enemy Stronghold.

#### Your Territory / Enemy Territory

- Home ground with moderate defender advantages.
- Contains three missions chosen by the defending player before the campaign starts.
- The Attacker selects which mission to play when this zone is contested.
- You are always the Defender in your Territory and the Attacker in the Enemy Territory.

#### Neutral Grounds

- The starting point of every Conquest Campaign.
- No defender bonuses (balanced combat).
- If the Mission has roles, a Roll-off determines Attacker and Defender.
- Contains three missions, played in sequence on repeated visits.

### Conquest Points (CP)

In addition to Victory Points (which convert to Gold for warband development), Conquest Campaigns award Conquest Points to track territorial conquest and to determine overall campaign victory. Victory Points and Conquest Points are tracked separately.

| Zone            | Attacker wins | Defender wins |
|-----------------|---------------|---------------|
| Neutral Grounds | 3 CP          | 3 CP          |
| Territory       | 5 CP          | 4 CP          |
| Stronghold      | Total Win     | 4 CP          |

### Campaign Flow

All Conquest Campaigns begin at Neutral Grounds. The campaign is a tug of war along the five zones: Enemy Stronghold, Enemy Territory, Neutral Grounds, Your Territory, Your Stronghold. After each battle the winner moves the campaign one zone toward the enemy's Stronghold, and the loser one zone back toward their own.

#### After each battle

1.  **Award Conquest Points** to the winner (table above).
2.  **Move the campaign.** Win: proceed one zone toward the enemy Stronghold. Lose: retreat one zone toward your own Stronghold. A win by the Attacker in a Stronghold is a Total Win instead and ends the campaign.
3.  **Conduct the After Battle Sequence.**
4.  **Set up the next Mission** (see Mission Selection).

A successful Stronghold defence pushes the campaign back into the defender's Territory; the defender then chooses the Territory Mission.

**A drawn battle**

If a Conquest battle ends in a draw, each player gains 1 CP and the campaign stays in the same zone. On Neutral Grounds the next Mission of the pool is played; in a Territory the Attacker chooses again.

Example A Conquest Campaign through the zones

Player A and Player B begin at Neutral Grounds. "Your" and "Enemy" below are seen from Player A's side.

1.  **Battle 1, Neutral Grounds.** A Roll-off decides Attacker and Defender; there are no Defender Bonuses. They play the first Base Mission of their pool. A wins: +3 CP (A 3, B 0). The campaign moves into B's Territory.
2.  **Battle 2, B's Territory.** A is the Attacker and chooses one of B's three Territory Missions. B reveals that mission's secret value and takes that many Defender Bonuses. B wins as Defender: +4 CP (A 3, B 4). The campaign moves back to Neutral Grounds.
3.  **Battle 3, Neutral Grounds.** They play the second Base Mission of the pool. A wins: +3 CP (A 6, B 4). The campaign moves into B's Territory again.
4.  **Battle 4, B's Territory.** A wins as Attacker: +5 CP (A 11, B 4). The campaign moves to B's Stronghold.
5.  **Battle 5, B's Stronghold.** B plays the chosen Stronghold Mission with 2 Defender Bonuses and holds: +4 CP (A 11, B 8). The campaign is pushed back into B's Territory, and this time B, not A, chooses the Territory Mission.
6.  **Battle 6, B's Territory.** A wins as Attacker: +5 CP (A 16, B 8) and advances to B's Stronghold once more.

If A now takes B's Stronghold, A wins the campaign at once. If both players agree to stop instead, A wins on Conquest Points, 16 to 8.

## Conquest Missions and Defender Bonuses

**At a glance**

- Before the campaign: agree on 3 Base Missions for Neutral Grounds; each player picks 3 Territory Missions and 1 Stronghold Mission.
- In a Territory the Attacker picks which of the defender's three Missions to play.
- The defender secretly marks those three Missions with 2, 1 and 0 Defender Bonuses.
- In a Stronghold the defender always takes 2 Defender Bonuses.

### Defender Bonuses

As a Defender, you may select from the Defender Bonus List below. A Defender Bonus may be selected twice unless stated otherwise.

| Zone            | Defender Bonuses |
|-----------------|------------------|
| Neutral Grounds | none             |
| Territory       | 0 – 2            |
| Stronghold      | 2                |

#### In a Stronghold

Before a Stronghold Mission is played, the Defender selects two Defender Bonuses from the Defender Bonus List.

#### In a Territory

1.  **Assign in secret.** Before a Territory Mission is selected, the Defender secretly assigns Defender Bonus values to their three Territory Missions: one mission gets 2, one gets 1, one gets 0. The value is how many Defender Bonuses will be available if that mission is played.
2.  **Attacker chooses.** The Attacker chooses the Territory Mission.
3.  **Reveal and select.** The Defender reveals the value assigned to that mission and selects that many Defender Bonuses from the Defender Bonus List.

#### Defender Bonus List

Defender Bonuses are effects of the Mission. They are not Traits and do not count toward the five-Trait limit.

| Bonus | Effect |
|----|----|
| **Fortified Position** | After deployment, place 3 additional Barricades anywhere on the battlefield except in the enemy Deployment Zone. |
| **Militia** | 50 Gold worth of reinforcements, this mission only. Does not add to the warband value. |
| **Home Advantage** | All models gain the Scout trait. |
| **Familiar Ground** | Models ignore Difficult Terrain. |
| **Arcane Wards** | Gains one additional Fate Die during the Fate Phase. |
| **Desperate Resolve** | Models in the deployment zone gain Relentless. Not in Siege of the Tower. |
| **Sacred Ground** | Models in the deployment zone gain Protected. Not in Siege of the Tower. |
| **Last Stand** | When a model is Defeated, roll a D6. On a 5+ it returns at the start of the next turn within 3″ of your Leader (on your table edge if you have no Leader), not in Base Contact with an enemy, with 1 Vitality (once per model). |
| **Siege Preparations** | Deploy 1 Stationary Weapon that can be used by a model within 1″ as a ranged weapon (immobile; RNG 18″, ATK 4, HTV 4+, DMG 4, CRT 6). |

Example Hidden bonus values

Player A attacks B's Territory. B's three Territory Missions are called T1, T2 and T3 here.

1.  B secretly notes: T1 = 2, T2 = 0, T3 = 1.
2.  A, the Attacker, suspects that T1 is well guarded and chooses T3.
3.  B reveals the value 1 and selects one bonus, Fortified Position: after deployment B places 3 additional Barricades anywhere except in A's Deployment Zone.
4.  Later A attacks B's Stronghold. B selects two Defender Bonuses and takes Militia twice, since a bonus may be selected twice: 2 × 50 = 100 Gold of reinforcements for this mission only, not added to B's warband value.

Because the values are hidden, the Attacker's choice is a gamble: the mission that looks best for the defender may carry no bonus at all.

## After Battle Sequence

**At a glance**

- Roll a D6 for every model that was Defeated and for every model that ended the Mission with Critical Injuries.
- Defeated: 1 dead, 2 to 4 Permanent Wound, 5 healed, 6 Survivor. Injured only: 1 to 4 the injuries persist, 5 healed, 6 Survivor.
- A Permanent Wound (D3: Head, Arm, Leg) stays until it is removed in the Hub.
- A Survivor rolls a D6 for a permanent bonus.

Between Missions in a campaign, warbands return to their Camp in the Grimheim Hub to recover, resupply and prepare for the next battle. Work through these steps in order:

1.  **Lasting Consequences.** Every model that was Defeated or remains Injured at the end of the Mission rolls on the appropriate table.
2.  **Heal and Recover.** Restore wounds, remove temporary effects and redistribute Equipment.
3.  **Visit the Hub.** Convert Victory Points to Gold and spend it (see Visit the Hub).

### Step 1 · Lasting Consequences

All models that were Defeated or remain Injured at the end of the Mission must roll on the appropriate table below to determine their fate.

#### Defeated models

When a model was Defeated during the Mission, roll a D6:

| D6 | Result | Effect |
|----|----|----|
| 1 | **Dead** | The model is permanently removed from your warband. |
| 2–4 | **Permanent Wound** | Roll on the Permanent Wounds table. |
| 5 | **Healed** | The model has recovered fully with no lasting effects. |
| 6 | **Survivor** | The model has recovered. Roll on the Survivor table for a bonus trait. |

#### Injured models

When a model ended the Mission with one or more Critical Injuries (see Vitality) but was not Defeated, roll a D6:

| D6 | Result | Effect |
|----|----|----|
| 1–4 | **Permanent Wound** | The injuries persist. |
| 5 | **Healed** | The model has recovered fully with no lasting effects. |
| 6 | **Survivor** | The model has recovered. Roll on the Survivor table for a bonus trait. |

#### Permanent Wounds table

Models that suffer Permanent Wounds gain lasting penalties. Roll a D3:

| D3 |  | Result | Effect |
|----|----|----|----|
| 1 | <img src="media/image/head-injury.png" width="40" /> | **Head** | HTV worsened by 1 (harder to hit enemies). |
| 2 | <img src="media/image/arm-injury.png" width="40" /> | **Arm** | ATK −1 (fewer attack dice). |
| 3 | <img src="media/image/leg-injury.png" width="40" /> | **Leg** | MOV −1 (reduced movement). |

A model may accumulate multiple Permanent Wounds over the course of a campaign. Track them on the model's card or in the Grimheim Companion.

**Healer:** Permanent Wounds can be removed by a Healer in the Hub (see Standard Locations; the Tavern and the Temple offer chancier cures).

#### Survivor table

Models that beat the odds gain experience from their near-death encounter. Roll a D6:

| D6 | Result | Effect |
|----|----|----|
| 1 | **Fleet-Footed** | MOV +1″ |
| 2 | **Stalwart** | Gains Stalwart. A model that already has Stalwart rolls again. |
| 3 | **Onslaught** | ATK +1 for the main-hand weapon. |
| 4 | **Expertise** | Main-hand HTV improved by 1. |
| 5–6 | **Veteran** | Gain the Veteran trait (free OVERWATCH after each activation). |

Survivor bonuses are permanent and remain with the model for the rest of the campaign. The Veteran and Stalwart traits count toward the five-Trait limit; a model that already has five Traits rolls again.

### Step 2 · Heal and Recover

![](media/image/sp_heal.png)

Restore wounds and remove temporary effects. What remains are the results of Step 1: Permanent Wounds, Survivor bonuses and the gaps left by the dead.

**Redistribute Equipment**

Equipment can be freely redistributed between the models of the warband. Equipment of Defeated models returns to the warband.

### Step 3 · Visit the Hub

The warband returns to the Hub to convert its Victory Points to Gold and spend it on new models, upgrades or special benefits. See Visit the Hub.

Example A full After Battle Sequence

Player A's warband of five Humans (MOV 5″) ends a Mission with 6 VP. The Captain, the Swordsman and a Spearman were Defeated. The Crossbowman survived with an Arm Injury from a critical hit. The Witchhunter is unharmed and rolls nothing.

SpearmanDefeated table \[1\] Dead. The Spearman is removed from the warband. SwordsmanDefeated table \[3\] Permanent Wound. D3 \[3\] Leg: MOV 5″ becomes 4″. CaptainDefeated table \[6\] Survivor. Survivor table \[2\] Stalwart: the Captain gains Stalwart and now rolls four Defense Dice. CrossbowmanInjured table \[5\] Healed. The Arm Injury does not persist.

1.  **Lasting Consequences:** as rolled above. The Swordsman keeps MOV 4″ until the Leg wound is removed.
2.  **Heal and Recover:** the four survivors restore their wounds, and temporary effects from the Mission end.
3.  **Visit the Hub:** Player A was not the Underdog, so 6 VP × 20 = 120 Gold.

The warband has lost the Spearman, gained a tougher Captain and carries one Leg wound into the Hub with 120 Gold to spend.

## Visit the Hub

**At a glance**

- Convert VP to Gold (20 per VP, Underdog 30) and sell looted Equipment for its Gold value.
- Each model may visit one location per After Battle Sequence.
- The Healer and the Tavern are always open. Merchants and Trainers are rolled for.
- Your faction may have locations of its own.

After determining the fate of all casualties, players may spend their earned Gold at the locations of their Camp in the Grimheim Hub.

### Gold

**Gold earned:** convert the Victory Points earned during the Mission to Gold at a rate of 20 Gold per VP.

**Underdog:** a player who received at least 5 VP fewer than any other player in the previous Mission converts Victory Points at 30 Gold per VP in this Mission (see Victory Rewards).

**Equipment:** a player may sell any Equipment that has been looted during a Mission for its corresponding Gold value.

Gold is the campaign's core resource. Use it to recruit new models, upgrade models, recover from lasting injuries and unlock faction-based bonuses.

**Key rule**

Each model may visit **one** location during the After Battle Sequence.

Example Healer and Tavern

After the sequence in the previous example, Player A has 120 Gold. During the Mission the Crossbowman looted a Healing Potion.

1.  A sells the looted Healing Potion for its Gold value of 12: 120 + 12 = 132 Gold.

The Swordsman could have tried Rest at the Tavern instead: half the price, but the wound only goes on a 4+.

The Swordsman is fit again, the next Mission brings an extra Side Quest, and 102 Gold remain. The Swordsman and the Witchhunter have used their one visit.
